# Plain look: the card

Briefs quote this card. Holds on top of `docs/look-base.md`. Reference: `SPEC.md`; anchors: `SHOWCASE.md`.

Plain is an editorial explainer: a data-journalism feature for adult readers. Calm, grown-up and visual. Not a cartoon, and not a grey page either.

Theme vs machinery. Plain changes the theme only: mascot, palette, cartoon art, gloss and lips, celebration flourishes, game chrome. All the machinery stays: every kit part and module (animation, tweens, draw-on, morph, camera, stage, rig, iso, physics, particles, WebGL, geo), at full power. College scenes are often more complex than school ones; use what the mechanism needs.

Keys: the OUTLINE header says `Look: plain` (Stage 1b); the module JSON carries `"look": "plain"` and `"gamification": false` (Stage 4). Classic lessons only: no story mode, no cast, comic panels or story narration. Optional per course in `bundle.json`: `"accent"` (the signature colour: `blue` default, `crimson`, `teal`, `indigo`, `ochre`, `green`) and `"simSetting": "casefile"` (a quiet casefile setting, SPEC sec 8). Type and tables are the engine's (SPEC sec 2): serif only at 24 px and up, the sans for everything smaller, no monospace; never set a font stack or table style in a scene. Leave both out unless the course brief asks.

1. The slide title states the point, not the topic: "A claim nobody can break is not a finding", not "Five parts of a hypothesis". At most 10 words, sentence case, no question marks on explain slides.
2. Every slide has something to look at. A slide with no scene, figure, graph or diagram is Text only and gives its key sentence or numbers one emphasis device: a pull quote (a `>` line), a takeaway (a bold sentence alone), a markdown table or worked steps (`docs/numericals.md` 3). Never two paragraphs alone.
3. The hero is the data or the mechanism: a chart, table, curve, flow, 2x2 grid, an annotated sentence, or a working model (a physical system, an organ, a market, a process over time). Take it from the kit (`briefs/reference/kit-parts.md`), never hand-drawn axes. A static figure is the slide's `figure` field (docs/figures.md; a flow, panels, a timeline) or `graph` for a plotted function, not a scene; `diagram` is geometry only.
4. Annotate the figure. Name the thing to look at on the figure itself: a callout with a leader line, an underline, a highlighter sweep (kit `SimAnnotate`). A caption under it says what to see, in one sentence.
5. Colour is meaning, and there is enough of it. Navy (`--sim-accent`) leads; the course signature colour (`--sim-annot`) marks what the beat is about and every annotation; `--sim-series-1..5` tell groups apart; grey `--sim-series-8` is the rest. Keep one group on one series across the course.
6. Flat fills, fine rims, small soft corners from `lookOf()` / `--look-*` only. No gloss, lips, cushion shade, gradient blobs or hardcoded radii.
7. Numbers carry units and the denominator they were computed on. Key readings are large (24-40 px).
8. No characters: no mascot, cast, sidekick, faces or cartoon icons, and no figure used as decoration. A person or animal may appear when it is the subject of the mechanism (a body in motion, a crowd moving through a space); the kit draws it neutral and faceless under plain. A population as data is `dotsSvg` or a count. Line icons from the kit are fine.
9. No playful leftovers: no arcade games, scores, hearts, stars, crowns, emoji, exclamation copy.
10. Motion explains, and there can be a lot of it: a bar grows, a line draws on, a camera moves to the detail, a process runs, a system settles. Physical motion is real physics (a spring springs, a ball bounces). What goes is decorative motion: UI pop and overshoot, sparkles, screen shake, idle wobble.
11. The find is quiet: the scene calls `celebrate()` once at the find; the kit draws a small tick (a stamp under the casefile setting). No burst, no confetti, no Quill.
12. Exercises, not games: a `mini-game@1` per lesson is optional (0 or 1) and is authored as an exercise (`docs/mini-games.md` "Exercise, not game"): one instruction, a live model the learner adjusts, an explicit check, feedback with the worked explanation. No timer, score, rounds counter or arcade framing. It fits the slide frame at 1280x800 and 390x844 with no page scroll. No arcade practice slides. A course that wants games picks look `quill`.
13. Earn every scene (`docs/pedagogy.md` "Earn every scene"). A scene only where its outline `Shows:` / `Without it:` lines are strong: a relationship that moves, a distribution that builds, a structure compared, a mechanism over time. No learner action and no changing quantity is decoration: make the slide Text only (rule 2). Spend scene effort where the idea is hard; reach for editorial panels first (`docs/pedagogy.md` "Dry, text-heavy subjects").
14. Numbers are worked, not stated (`docs/numericals.md` 2): numbered rows (label, big serif formula with its numbers, one gloss line), a stacked fraction for multiply or divide, a "Show worked arithmetic" reveal, numeric entry with a check where computing is the idea.
15. Actionable things look actionable (`docs/numericals.md` 4): a button-like control with a visible picked state, a prompt that says the move, the result beside the control. Never bare text that happens to be clickable.
16. No game words. Learner-visible text and titles never say game, play, level, score, badge, XP or challenge (as a label): say exercise, task, practice. `prose-lint` flags them (prose, block strings, scene strings). Under plain the engine labels a `mini-game@1` "Exercise" (no badge, no box).
17. Clarity beats brevity. Keep the full explanation a first-time learner needs; split it over beats rather than cutting it.
