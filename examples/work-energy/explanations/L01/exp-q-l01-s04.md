# Reading the Work off a Force-Displacement Graph

<!-- STEP -->
## Draw it first

Draw force on the vertical axis and displacement on the horizontal axis. The force rises in a straight line from 0 N to 50 N over the first metre. It stays flat at 50 N for the next two metres. Then it falls in a straight line back to 0 N over the last metre.

The shape is a **trapezium**: a rectangle with a triangle at each end.

<!-- STEP -->
## Split it into shapes you know

The area of a trapezium is easy to get wrong, so cut it into three simple shapes:

- **0 to 1 m:** a triangle, base 1 m, height 50 N
- **1 m to 3 m:** a rectangle, 2 m by 50 N
- **3 m to 4 m:** a triangle, base 1 m, height 50 N

$$\text{area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1 \times 50 \;+\; 2 \times 50 \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1 \times 50$$

<!-- STEP -->
## Add them up

$$25\ \mathrm{J} \;+\; 100\ \mathrm{J} \;+\; 25\ \mathrm{J} \;=\; 150\ \mathrm{J}$$

The area under a force-displacement graph is the work done. This is true whether the line is straight, flat or curved.
