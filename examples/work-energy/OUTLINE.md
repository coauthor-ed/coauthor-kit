# OUTLINE - work-energy (Chapter 7, "Work, Energy, and Simple Machines")

Working file, not published content (no `blockRef`, not subject to the `ai-draft` rule). Stage 1b output: outline + pedagogy design only. Slide *text*, worked-example *values* and figure *detail* are not written here - this file says **which** beat, **which** role, **which** scene, and points at `FIGURES.md` / the chapter text for the content. `AGENTS.md` rule 4 still binds whoever writes the prose: the textbook's own numbers are for question design, not lesson prose.

## Conventions used below

- **Role** is one of `hook` / `predict` / `explain` / `example`. `hook (closing)` = the cliffhanger that opens the next lesson; `explain (challenge slot)` = the lesson's one `mini-game@1`, placed after the mechanism is taught and before the closing cliffhanger (`docs/mini-games.md`).
- **Call** is `sim` (scene filename + base class), `image` (FIGURES.md label), or `neither` (with a reason). Nearly every slide is a simulation (`AGENTS.md` rule 10). Base class by content shape: `CanvasSimulation` for continuous motion / physics, `DomSimulation` for panels, ledgers, taxonomies, tables, timelines.
- **One `poe-predict@1` per lesson**, always first or second, always `deferVerdict: true`, with the reveal on the *immediately* following block (`content-block@1` with `poeRole: "explain"`, or `poe-explain@1`). No slide is ever inserted between a deferred predict and its reveal. `poe-predict@1` does take a `simulation` (`docs/simulation-authoring.md` section 4): by default the scene appears only *after* the learner answers (the POE "observe" beat); `"role": "setup"` mounts it at once beside the prompt, with no beats, for a scene the question refers to. Where a predict slide below names a scene, Stage 2 picks per slide: `role: "setup"` if the prompt needs the scene to be asked (a snapshot to predict from - and then the scene must not show the answer), otherwise the default after-answer scene, in which case the reveal block need not mount it again. An `image` and a `simulation` cannot both be on one block.
- **A scene named on two slides** is one file mounted twice, memory-less each time (cross-mount rule in `docs/simulation-authoring.md`) - marked "*(reuse, independent mounts)*". It must never assume it remembers the earlier mount.
- Every block gets a sibling `.md` prose file via `markdownRef`/`promptRef` (`AGENTS.md` rule 2) and `"authoring": { "status": "ai-draft" }`.
- `g = 10 m s^-2` unless a slide says otherwise; the chapter's own examples all assume it.
- **Arcade practice slides** (`a01`, one per lesson in eight lessons, L03 has none) are optional timed drills of what was already taught, on a plain `content-block@1` (one line of prose) with an `Arcade*Scene` and a data deck (`docs/arcade-games.md`); not the lesson's `mini-game@1`, never graded. Numbered `a01` so they can sit anywhere without renumbering the `s` slides (blocks are `blk-l0N-a01`). Archetypes rotate across the chapter (route, sort, sort, match, order, sort, route, sort, judge) and no two neighbouring games share archetype and skin; the decks grow lesson to lesson only with things taught before the slide (`taught by`).

## The chapter's arc

The chapter's own front page already plants a question, and the textbook answers it on page 127. That is the spine: **L01 parks three playground questions and never answers them; L05 pays them off in one line, `v = sqrt(2gh)`.** Inside that frame, a single raised ball carries the middle of the chapter - lifted (`Fig. 7.2`), given a stored debt (L04), released (L05) - and the machine half of the chapter reuses the one number established in L01: `W = F x s`. A machine never changes that product, it only trades the two factors; that is why "machines do not create energy" can be *derived* in L09 instead of asserted.

Each lesson's closing slide is worded to be the next lesson's opening question. Forward-reference check done end to end (`AGENTS.md` rule 7): L01 uses only force, displacement and work; `energy`, `kinetic`, `potential`, `power`, `mechanical advantage` and the machine vocabulary each first appear in the lesson that teaches them; L02's two-column split is labelled `MOTION` / `POSITION` and deliberately does **not** use the words "kinetic" or "potential" before L03/L04 do.

Lesson opening order: eight of the nine lessons (L01, L03-L09) open on a hook slide (s01) and put the predict at s02; only L02 opens on its predict. In L01 the hook poses the chapter-level puzzle and attaches a live scene, and the wager itself - the brick wall - is s02, answered in s03. The intent of "the predict comes before the first content block" is kept (nothing is taught before the wager); only a framing beat sits in front of it. Hook-then-predict is the norm here, which is a template risk: a Stage 2 agent should vary how each hook is built, and may open a lesson straight on its predict where the material is better served.

## L01 - Pushing the wall (7.1 Work Done by a Constant Force)

Arc: the chapter's three playground questions get parked and left hanging; the lesson earns the one number that will answer them. Opens on the puzzle, spends its middle on the day's best dead end (you can be exhausted and have done nothing), and closes on the fact that a negative number can be *saved*.

## L02 - Work becomes a bank balance (7.2 The Work-Energy Theorem, 7.3 Forms of Energy)

Arc: the cliffhanger from L01 is cashed immediately - a thrown ball is still carrying the thrower's work - and the chapter's biggest vocabulary slide (seven forms of energy) is given a job: to show that mechanical energy is one column in a much bigger table, which is the only honest reason to care about it.

Is the energy that scattered the stumps **used up** by the throw, or is it **still in the ball** on the way? Options: spent by the throw / still in the ball / split between the fielder and the ball. (Scene placement on this predict - `role: "setup"` or the default after-answer mount - is a Stage 2 call per the conventions block; the throw itself must not show the charge meter before the learner answers.)
- **s02** - explain (verdict) - `content-block@1` + `poeRole: "explain"` - **sim** `BallChargeScene` (Canvas) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.8* - the verdict, then the mechanism: a charge meter loads into the ball at the throw, rides with it, and drains into the stumps at impact. The three panels of `Fig. 7.8` (throw, wicket, falling pot) are the source's own sequence - reuse that order.
- **s03** - explain - `content-block@1` - **image** *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.1* - the three vignettes - food walking you, electricity turning the fan, fuel moving the car - as the answer to "where did the work in your muscles come from in the first place". - **s04** - explain - `content-block@1` - **sim** `WorkEnergyLedgerScene` (Dom) - the theorem as a two-column account the student fills in: work done on one side, change in energy on the other. - **s06** - explain - `content-block@1` - **sim** `EnergyFormsBoardScene` (Dom) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.10* - the seven forms as tappable panels, plus a conversion control: pick a transformation (bulb, water heater, bell, your legs) and watch energy move between columns. ## L03 - The debt of motion (7.4.1 Kinetic Energy)

Arc: L02 left two columns and no numbers. L03 prices the first one, and its whole lesson is one dead end - the one almost everyone walks into - that speed is squared, not linear. It closes by pointing out that `1/2 m v^2` never mentions height, and a ball on a table can still break a tile.

- **s01** - hook - `content-block@1` - **sim** `SameSpeedTwoBallsScene` (Canvas) - a 20 g table-tennis ball and a 2 kg iron ball rolling side by side at the same speed; the question is which one has more to spend. Wrong idea named first, per `docs/voice-and-tone.md`: the heavy one, obviously. The scene's mass dial is what makes the answer feel earned rather than asserted.
- **s02** - predict - `poe-predict@1`, `deferVerdict: true` - **sim** `SpeedDoublesScene` (Canvas) - same mass, speed doubled: how many times the energy? Options: twice / four times / unchanged. Nobody is born knowing this, and the wrong answer is so common that the reveal is worth its own slide.
- **s03** - explain (verdict) - `content-block@1` + `poeRole: "explain"` - **sim** `KineticEnergyScene` (Canvas) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.11* - the verdict, then the derivation as a live ladder: `F` and `s` dials, `a`, and the `1/2` arriving from the `2a` in the kinematic equation rather than being asserted. The `Fig. 7.11` entry records the algebra and the initial/final car states; the scene should feel like watching that car go from "initial velocity = 0" to "final velocity = v" with a meter filling, not like reading four lines of working.
- **s04** - explain - `content-block@1` - **sim** `KineticEnergyScene` (Canvas, reuse, independent mounts) - the naming beat: **"this is called kinetic energy"**, `K = 1/2 m v^2`, the joule, no direction, and the three sign cases (positive work raises it, negative work lowers it, no work leaves it alone). Pause & Ponder 5 lives here - kinetic energy does not change with position at constant velocity - because it is the same idea stated negatively.
- **s05** - example - `content-block@1` - **sim** `DoublesWorkoutScene` (Canvas) - Ex 7.4, the doubling that is really a quadrupling, done as a live `m`/`v` panel rather than a proof. The teaching point is the *squared*, and the only way to make `v^2` stick is to make the student watch the number change when they touch the slider.
- **s06** - example - `content-block@1` - **sim** `CricketBallKScene` (Canvas) - Ex 7.5, the bowler's 0.2 kg at 154.8 km h^-1 (values in the chapter text). The scene's real job is the **unit conversion**: km/h and m/s side by side, live, so `43 m s^-1` is a thing the student did rather than a thing they were told.
- **s07** - example - `content-block@1` - **sim** `JetCarrierDeckScene` (Canvas) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.12* (Ex 7.6) - the arresting cable, a `K` bar draining from its initial value to nothing over 100 m against a 367500 N backward force. This is L01's negative work returning as a real stopping event; the scene should let the student pick the arrival speed rather than read it, since the chapter's own answer is a back-solve from zero kinetic energy.
- **s08** - explain - `content-block@1` - **sim** `EqualKTwoMassesScene` (Canvas) - Pause & Ponder 4, two objects of mass `m` and `4m` with the same kinetic energy: the live `m` and `v` dials show that the heavier one must be slower, and by how much. A prediction-shaped slide with the answer built in; let the student find the ratio before the readout states it.
- **s09** - explain (challenge slot) - `mini-game@1` - **sim** `KineticRampGameScene` (Canvas) - "Load the Launcher" - push a crate along a track with a chosen force and distance to hit a target kinetic energy exactly. Tie **L2**: the crate's own `K` is the scoreboard, so the only way to land on the target is `1/2 m v^2` done right - and stopping the crate early (negative work) is a legitimate second solution, which keeps the L01 sign rules alive.
- **s10** - hook (closing) - `content-block@1` - **sim** `NoHeightInTheFormulaScene` (Dom) - the formula on screen with its two ingredients named, and the ball on a table sitting there with nothing - closes on the question L04 opens with: this number says nothing about height, and yet height can hurt. **What is height keeping?**

## L04 - The debt of height (7.4.2 Potential Energy)

Arc: L03 priced motion; L04 prices position, and the interesting part is that it is not one kind of debt but a family of them - deformed springs, separated magnets, separated charges, a ball held off the ground. The lesson's dead end is the "work against friction is not stored" counter-case, which stops the idea from becoming "any effort is banked".

- **s01** - hook - `content-block@1` - **sim** `SlingshotBandScene` (Canvas) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.13* - pull the band, watch a stored meter fill, release, watch it empty into the pellet. The stored meter is the hook; do not name the quantity yet. The chapter's own bridge is the Grade 6 coin stack on a rubber band, which the student has met before - say so, it costs one sentence and makes the section feel like a return rather than a new topic.
- **s02** - predict - `poe-predict@1`, `deferVerdict: true` - **sim** `SlingshotBandScene` (Canvas, reuse, independent mounts) - pull the band back twice as far: how much energy is stored - twice as much, four times, or the same? Correct answer is four times, and "twice" is the wrong answer the whole world gives.
- **s03** - explain (verdict) - `content-block@1` + `poeRole: "explain"` - **sim** `SpringStagesScene` (Canvas) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.14* - the three stages the source draws (original, compressed, back to original) as three live states with the stored meter and the ball's speed both visible. The textbook's `Ready to Go Beyond` note - internal forces undo the deformation, and that undoing is where the work comes out - is the sentence that makes this mechanical rather than magical; keep it in the prose.
- **s04** - explain - `content-block@1` - **sim** `SystemSeparationScene` (Dom) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.15* (magnets, charges) and *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.16* (Earth-ball) - the generalisation: energy stored by a *system* because of how its parts sit relative to each other. Three panels the student drags apart and together, with the stored value rising and falling. `Fig. 7.16` earns a specific point here that the other two do not: gravity is mutual, so the energy belongs to the Earth-and-ball pair, and we only call it the ball's because the Earth is too heavy to move - that is the chapter's own argument and it is the honest one.
- **s05** - example - `content-block@1` - **sim** `SandCraterScene` (Canvas) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.17* (Activity 7.1) - two balls dropped from two heights into loose sand, crater depths compared live. The activity is the chapter's own experiment and the scene should let the student choose the second drop height themselves; the surprise is the *proportionality*, and it is only visible if the drop height is a dial.
- **s06** - explain - `content-block@1` - **sim** `PotentialDerivationScene` (Dom) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.18* - `U = mgh` built from `W = F x s` with `F = mg` and `s = h`, plus the choice that has to be made out loud: PE is set to zero on the ground, so every later number depends on where you put the zero. The source's own caveat - the expression is valid only near the Earth's surface - is not decoration and should be one beat here, not a footnote.
- **s07** - explain - `content-block@1` - **sim** `StoredEnergyKindsScene` (Dom) - the family sorted: stored by **deformation** (spring, band, bow) versus stored by **separation** (magnets, charges, Earth-ball), and then the counter-case that stops the idea running away - work done against friction stores nothing. This is the chapter's `Ready to Go Beyond` note and it is the best dead end in the lesson, because a student who has just watched four kinds of energy get banked will absolutely believe effort is banked in general.
- **s08** - example - `content-block@1` - **sim** `FielderThrowScene` (Canvas) - Ex 7.7, a 200 g ball thrown 10 m up in celebration, `U = 20 J` at the top (values in the chapter text). The point of the example is the asymmetry: the ball has plenty of energy at the top of the throw and none of it is kinetic yet.
- **s09** - explain - `content-block@1` - **sim** `HeightOnlyScene` (Canvas) - the elevator and the staircase (chapter review Q5) as two routes to the same floor in one scene, with the gain in potential energy identical on both. This is the first appearance of the chapter's deepest structural idea - the path does not appear in the answer - and it is deliberately placed *before* conservation so that L05 reads as the explanation of something the student has already seen.
- **s10** - example - `content-block@1` - **sim** `MoonGravityScene` (Canvas) - chapter review Q12: the same throw goes 8 m on Earth and how far on the Moon at `1/6 g`? A `g` dial and two arcs. It is the one application that shows `g` is *inside* `mgh`, and it costs one slide to make that unmissable.
- **a01** - practice - `content-block@1` - **sim** `ChapterFormulasRangeScene` (ArcadeMatch/range) - deck: quantity <-> what it is (`W = F x s` work, `K = 1/2 m v^2` kinetic energy, `U = m g h` gravitational potential energy, joule as the unit of all three, each with one distinguishing clue such as *squared* or *needs a height*); distractors come from the other pairs. Taught by L01 s07, L03 s04, L04 s06. Why here: by now three formulas that look alike are in play and the lesson's `mgh` derivation has just landed; this drills which is which before power and machines add more.
- **s11** - explain (challenge slot) - `mini-game@1` - **sim** `StoredEnergyGameScene` (Canvas) - "Stack the Debt" - lift a load to a target height using a combination of elastic and gravitational stores, and deliver exactly a target energy to a receiver. Tie **L2**: the receiver is a live `K` meter, so only a correct `U = mgh` (plus whatever the spring contributed) can charge it, and a friction-only route scores zero by construction.
- **s12** - hook (closing) - `content-block@1` - **sim** `TwoAccountsScene` (Dom) - the ball on a shelf: one account filled, the other empty, and a single "combine" control that has not been pressed yet - closes on the question L05 opens with: the ball has two debts now. **What happens when it is let go - and does the total ever change?**

## L05 - The switch that never runs out (7.4.3 Conservation of Mechanical Energy)

Arc: the chapter's payoff. s03 proves the total does not change; s06 walks back to the playground from L01 s01 and answers all three parked questions in one line, `v = sqrt(2gh)`. Everything after that is the honesty slide - real coasts, real ramps, and a coconut in sand - because a chapter that ends on "energy is conserved" without saying *conserved by what, and what happens when something else is acting* has taught a law with the conditions left off. Closes by noticing that every answer this lesson produced was a **speed**, and a speed says nothing about how long you took.

- **s01** - hook - `content-block@1` - **sim** `TwoDebtsScene` (Dom) - the two accounts of L04 s12, now on a single live object with a release control. The press is the whole slide; the prose should be one sentence.
- **s02** - predict - `poe-predict@1`, `deferVerdict: true` - **sim** `FreeFallLedgerScene` (Canvas) - a ball dropped from 2 m. Halfway down, at 1 m, what fraction of its mechanical energy is kinetic? Options: half / a quarter / all of it. Correct is half, and the reason - `v` goes with the square root of the fall, not with the fall - is exactly what the next slide has to show, so the wrong answer here is worth more than the right one.
- **s03** - explain (verdict) - `content-block@1` + `poeRole: "explain"` - **sim** `FreeFallLedgerScene` (Canvas) - *see FIGURES.md: Fig. 7.19* - the verdict, then the A-B-C ladder from the source: a `PE` bar draining, a `KE` bar filling, and a **total bar that does not move**. ## Chapter-wide notes for later stages

**The playground loop.** L01 s01 poses three questions and must not answer any of them; L05 s06 and s07 answer all three; L09 s09 returns to the image a third time. **Glossary.** This chapter carries two names that genuinely need the treatment `docs/glossary.md` describes: **fulcrum** (Latin *fulcrum*, "prop, bedpost") and **lever** (Latin *levare*, "to raise"). **Where the textbook's numbers may and may not appear.** Chapter and worked-example values (Ex 7.1-7.13, Activities 7.1-7.5, review Q5-Q15, and the values recorded in each `FIGURES.md` entry) are for **question design**. **Open calls a reviewer may want to overturn.** (1) L01's hook-before-predict ordering, argued above.
