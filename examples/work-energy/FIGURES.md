## Fig. 7.7 (p.4 / book p.119) - Examples of (a) positive, and (b) negative work done on object
- Used by: L01 s09, L01 s10
- Inspected: rasterized + viewed directly
- Description: Two-part illustration distinguishing positive work from negative work:
  - (a) Positive work done by the boy on the wheelchair: A young boy pushing a wheelchair carrying an elderly woman to the right. Above them are two parallel horizontal arrows pointing right: top arrow labeled "Force $\longrightarrow$", bottom arrow labeled "Displacement $\longrightarrow$". Because force and displacement are in the same direction ($\theta = 0^\circ$), work is positive ($W > 0$).
  - (b) Negative work done by the goalkeeper on the ball: A goalkeeper in pink and blue diving upward and to the right in front of a white goal net, stopping an incoming soccer ball with her gloved hands. Above her are two opposing horizontal arrows: top arrow labeled "Force $\longrightarrow$" (hands pushing forward/right against the ball), bottom arrow labeled "Displacement $\longleftarrow$" (her hands are pushed backwards/left by the ball as it comes to a stop). Because the force applied by the goalkeeper on the ball is opposite to the ball's displacement ($\theta = 180^\circ$), the goalkeeper does negative work on the ball ($W < 0$).
  - Companion "Note" box highlights Newton's third law: The ball exerts an equal and opposite force on the goalkeeper; since that force and the goalkeeper's hand displacement are in the same direction, the ball does positive work on the goalkeeper.
- Numbers/values: (Worked Example 7.2 on same page): Force applied by goalkeeper $= 200\text{ N}$, hand displacement $= 15\text{ cm} = 0.15\text{ m}$ backwards (displacement along force $=-0.15\text{ m}$), Work done $= 200\text{ N} \times (-0.15\text{ m}) = -30\text{ J}$.

## Fig. 7.11 (p.6 / book p.121) - Calculating change in kinetic energy using the work-energy theorem
- Used by: L03 s03
- Inspected: rasterized + viewed directly
- Description: Schematic of a blue passenger car undergoing linear acceleration along a horizontal road under a constant forward force $F$:
  - Left state (Initial): Blue car stationary; labeled "Initial velocity = 0", "Initial Kinetic energy = 0".
  - A horizontal arrow labeled $F$ points right along the ground line.
  - Displacement dimension arrow below labeled "Displacement along the force = $s$".
  - Center label: "$\text{Work done by force } W = F \times s$".
  - Right state (Final): Same blue car moving to the right; labeled "Final velocity = $v$", "Final Kinetic energy = $K$".
  - Text derives: $v^2 = u^2 + 2as \implies s = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a}$. With $F = ma$, $W = F \times s = ma \times \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a} = \frac{1}{2}m(v^2 - u^2)$. For $u = 0$, $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.
- Numbers/values: $u = 0$, $v$, $F$, $s$, $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.

## Fig. 7.19 (p.11 / book p.126) - An object falling freely due to gravity (Conservation of mechanical energy)
- Used by: L05 s03
- Inspected: rasterized + viewed directly
- Description: A vertical coordinate diagram tracking a falling block of mass $m$ at three key positions to prove that total mechanical energy $E = PE + KE$ is constant throughout free fall:
  - Ground baseline labeled "C".
  - Top position labeled "A, $t = 0$": Blue cube at maximum height $h$. Downward gravity arrow labeled $mg$. At A: $u = 0$, $PE = mgh$, $KE = 0$, $E_A = mgh$.
  - Intermediate position labeled "B, $t = t$": Semi-transparent blue cube with label "$m$" inside, at height $h'$ above ground C. A vertical dotted line represents the fall trajectory from A to B.
  - Dimension markers on the left:
    - Black vertical bar spanning ground C to point A labeled "$h$".
    - Green vertical bar spanning ground C to point B labeled "$h'$".
    - Red vertical bar spanning point A to point B labeled "$(h - h')$" (the distance fallen).
  - Kinematics at B: $v = gt$, fall distance $h - h' = \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \implies h' = h - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$.
    - $PE_B = mgh' = mgh - \frac{1}{2}mg^2t^2$.
    - $KE_B = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m(gt)^2 = \frac{1}{2}mg^2t^2$.
    - Total mechanical energy $E_B = PE_B + KE_B = mgh$.
  - At bottom C ($h=0$): $v = \sqrt{2gh}$, $PE_C = 0$, $KE_C = \frac{1}{2}m(2gh) = mgh$, $E_C = mgh$.
- Numbers/values: Equations 7.9a-c, 7.10a-c. Total energy is invariant at $mgh$. Worked Example 7.7 on same page: mass $m = 200\text{ g} = 0.2\text{ kg}$, $h = 10\text{ m}$, $g = 10\text{ m s}^{-2}$, $PE = mgh = 0.2 \times 10 \times 10 = 20\text{ J}$.

- Used by: L07 s07
